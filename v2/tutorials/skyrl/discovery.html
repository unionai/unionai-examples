Discovery guide: teams training agents with RL

SkyRLHarborAgentic RL Questions that surface the problems Union solves — and what to say when you hear them.

Who this is for. Teams running reinforcement learning on LLM agents: the model attempts a task inside an isolated environment (a "sandbox"), gets graded, and the training loop nudges the model toward higher scores. SkyRL, Harbor, veRL and similar frameworks are the tell. Typical setup: a Ray GPU cluster for training + inference, hundreds of sandboxes (Modal, Daytona, e2b, Docker) running in parallel, and an external LLM used as a judge.
  • What we do NOT claim: we don't make their training step or GPU code faster. That part is already well engineered inside the framework.
  • What we DO solve: losing hours of paid work when a run crashes; not being able to see or debug which attempt hung; sandbox and judge cost that gets thrown away; no way to re-score or branch a run without redoing it; and the operations around the run (data, images, evals, experiments) on Kubernetes.
  • How to use: ask the five must-ask questions first — one per pain (recovery, scale of loss, observability, cost, cold start). The sections below go deeper on each. Write down numbers — they are the business case.
Five questions you must not skip — one per pain. Get answers to these and the rest of the guide is optional.
  1. Recovery — "When the training run dies, what do you lose, and what does getting back look like — who does it, and how long until training is moving again?" The framework has no fault tolerance; today recovery is a person relaunching from the last checkpoint. Listen for hours of lost work and a human in the loop.
  2. Scale of loss — "How many attempts run at once, and how long does one take?" Multiply them: that's the paid work thrown away on every crash (128 attempts × 8 min ≈ 17 sandbox-hours, plus GPU time). Say the number back to them.
  3. Observability — "When a run stalls or one attempt misbehaves, how do you find out which one, and where are its logs?" Silent hangs are the #1 failure mode; today it's one giant driver log. Listen for "we grep" or "we don't know until the morning."
  4. Cost — "What do sandboxes and the judge cost per training step, and how much of that is attempts you end up discarding — timeouts, errors, judge failures, retries that pay twice?" Listen for "we don't break it out" — that's spend nobody is defending.
  5. Cold start — "How long from 'attempt starts' to the agent's first action, and is that mostly pulling a container image?" Decides whether to pitch hosting their sandboxes: multi-GB image pulls per attempt is our win; a vendor that restores in 2 s is not.

1. Failure & recovery the pain nobody else fixes

Their framework has no fault tolerance: when the training driver dies, every attempt in flight and every finished-but-not-yet-trained batch is gone. Restart tools from Ray, Anyscale or SkyPilot only bring them back to the last checkpoint. Union keeps the finished attempts.

AskListen forWhat we'd propose
When the training driver dies — out of memory, a network timeout, one bad attempt — what do you lose, and how do you get back? MUST ASK"Everything in flight. We restart from the last checkpoint by hand."Each attempt becomes a durable Union task: finished attempts survive a crash and are reused when the run resumes.
How many attempts are in flight at once, and how long is a typical one? MUST ASK"128 × 5–20 minutes."Loss per crash = attempts × duration × (sandbox $ + GPU time). Say the number back to them.
How often does a run die per day, and who notices?"Someone checks the dashboard in the morning."Automatic retries, alerts (Slack/email/webhook), one-click recover.
How long does saving a checkpoint block training?"Minutes; one 10-minute save crashed a run."Saves off the critical path; every checkpoint tracked as an artifact.
If only the training loop crashes, do you lose the whole GPU cluster — engines, loaded models and all?"Yes, everything restarts together."Run each training step as its own Union task on a long-lived, reusable Ray cluster: the step is the recovery boundary, the cluster and loaded models stay warm across steps and across a driver restart (verified on a Union demo cluster).

2. Hangs & visibility detection, not just retry

The most common failure in these systems is a silent hang — the run just stops, no error. Today it's one giant log file. Union turns each attempt into a tracked unit with its own logs and a deadline, so a hang becomes "attempt 37 on task X timed out" instead of a mystery.

AskListen forWhat we'd propose
Have you had a run that just… stopped — no error, nothing progressing?"Yes, more than once." (It's a known open issue in SkyRL.)Per-attempt timeouts; hangs are localized and named; the run keeps going.
When one attempt misbehaves, can you find its logs, its sandbox, its judge transcript? MUST ASK"We grep the driver log."Every attempt has its own logs, inputs, outputs and lineage in the UI; live run reports.
Which tasks or environments are slowest, most expensive, or fail most?"Not really tracked."Per-environment metrics come for free from tracked tasks.
What happens to the whole run when a single attempt throws an error?"The step fails" / "the process exits."Failed attempts are records, not crashes; retry or skip per attempt.

3. Sandbox economics where the money goes

Each attempt runs in a rented sandbox. When runs crash or attempts time out, that spend is thrown away. If they're on Kubernetes, Union can run the sandboxes next to the GPUs — no vendor bill, no quota, no data egress — and start them faster if their images are large.

AskListen forWhat we'd propose
What's the sandbox bill per training step, and how much of it is attempts that got thrown away (timeouts, errors, judge failures)? MUST ASK"We don't break it out."Durable attempts + rate limiting so paid work isn't discarded.
Do you hit vendor concurrency or quota limits? What does training do when you do?"Generation stalls; we dial down parallelism."Built-in queues with concurrency caps — explicit backpressure instead of stalls.
Time from "attempt starts" to the agent's first action — how much is image pull? MUST ASK"A minute or more for cold environments."Measured on Union: ~90 s to pull a 10 GB image cold vs under 1 second to give an attempt its files from a forked volume.
What differs between your environment images — the code, or the files inside?"Mostly the files."One shared image + per-environment data on a Union volume; each attempt gets an instant private copy; "reset" is free.
Where does traffic between the GPU cluster and the sandboxes flow, and who pays the egress?"Different cloud / different region."Run sandboxes on the same Kubernetes cluster as training.
Do sandboxes leak when a run fails or someone hits Ctrl-C?"We clean them up by hand."Task lifecycle owns teardown.

4. The judge the external LLM that scores attempts

Scores usually come from calling an external model (Gemini, GPT). When that call fails or is rate-limited, most frameworks silently record a score of zero — corrupting training — and every retry pays for the call again.

AskListen forWhat we'd propose
What does a judge rate-limit or timeout do to the score?"It gets a zero."Judge calls are retried with backoff and memoized — a retry never re-bills or re-scores.
What's the judge spend per step, and do you ever pay twice for the same attempt?"Every retry re-calls the judge."Keep Gemini. Put the call behind a rate limit and memoize it per attempt so retries and forks never re-bill.
Can you re-score last week's attempts with a new rubric without regenerating them?"No — we'd rerun everything."Fork the run: generation is reused, only scoring re-runs. Judge cost only. This is usually the "I didn't know that was possible" moment.

5. Everything around the run data, images, evals, experiments

SkyRL has no Kubernetes support and nothing for the pipeline around a training run. This is Union's home turf and carries no risk to their training code.

AskListen forWhat we'd propose
How do you pin the exact task set to a run? Could you reproduce last week's run?"A file path and a git hash, roughly."Versioned, cached data prep; the run records exactly which dataset version it used.
Who builds and pre-pulls your environment images, and how do you find out one is broken?"Mid-run, as failed attempts."Image build/pre-pull as a tracked, cached step that fails early.
How do you evaluate a checkpoint on held-out tasks?"Separate script, by hand."Same attempt code, run as an eval fan-out against a served checkpoint; results stored as artifacts.
How many experiments run at once, and do they interfere with each other?"They share the cluster and the sandbox quota."Isolated runs, each with its own pools; sweeps as first-class runs.
Can you branch a run at step N with a different setting and compare?"Restart from a checkpoint by hand and regenerate everything."Run fork reuses everything up to the branch point.

Suggested order

  1. The five must-ask questions first. Recovery and the attempts × duration number anchor everything else.
  2. Section 2. The silent-hang question almost always lands.
  3. Section 3. Cold-start time and "code vs files" decide whether to pitch hosting their sandboxes.
  4. Section 4. Lead with re-scoring old attempts with a new rubric.
  5. Sections 5–6 last, once the pain is on the table.

Glossary

Attempt / trial / rollout
One try by the model at one task, inside a sandbox. Minutes each for agent tasks.
Sandbox / environment
The isolated container an attempt runs in (Modal, Daytona, e2b, Docker). Often one container image per task "world".
Judge / verifier / reward
How an attempt gets its score: tests that run in the sandbox, plus often an external LLM grading against a rubric.
Driver
The single process that orchestrates the whole training run. When it dies, in-flight work dies with it.
Checkpoint
A saved copy of the model and training state; the only thing a restart can resume from today.
Fully async / staleness
Generating attempts and training on them at the same time; "staleness" is how many steps old an attempt may be before it's used.
Fork
Union: recover an old run with changed code and/or changed inputs; everything whose code and inputs didn't change is reused, the rest re-runs. Also works on volumes: an instant private copy of a filesystem.
Ray / KubeRay
The cluster framework these trainers run on. Union launches and retires Ray clusters on Kubernetes as part of a run, and can keep one cluster alive across many tasks.
